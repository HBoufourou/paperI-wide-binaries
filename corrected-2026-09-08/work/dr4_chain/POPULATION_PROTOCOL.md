## Invariant boundaries and inner-orbit support

The scalar interpolant supplies a conservative bound Cmax≥max(-rΨ) on its entire closed domain. A convex Bernstein-coefficient bound is sufficient; it certifies this interpolant, not the exact continuum QUMOND solution. With Lz=(r×v)z, E≥Lz²/(2r²)−Cmax/r. Require E<−Cmax/rmax and the internal barrier radius

rlo=Lz²/[Cmax+√(Cmax²+2 E Lz²)] > 0.035.

Reject nonpositive/invalid discriminants and vanishing Lz. Thus orbits conserving E and Lz cannot cross rmin=0.03 or rmax=30. The mask is a function of integrals of the motion, so the parent remains stationary in the time-independent interpolant. Its existence and conservation must be checked numerically before scientific observation. This is a specific restricted orbital family, not an empirical determination of the true eccentricity distribution.

For a system with one inner companion, require inner apocentre a(1+e)<rlo*rM/20. This is a conservative prescribed hierarchy relative to the outer barrier, not a universal triple-stability theorem. Treat the compact inner system as a monopole for the outer force; omit its quadrupole, secular coupling, tides and evolution. State those approximations explicitly. At 66-month epochs, propagate the outer barycentres through the scalar-potential gradient, and the internal orbit with the stated Newtonian Kepler model.
